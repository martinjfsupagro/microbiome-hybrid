RESULTS — Technical reproducibility (draft paragraph, ~150 words)

Technical variation was negligible relative to biological variation. In a PERMANOVA
on Bray-Curtis distances with the technical terms entered first — the conservative
ordering, which assigns them the maximum variance they can explain — library
preparation accounted for 0.03% of the variance (R2 = 0.0003, p = 0.63) and
sequencing run within library for 0.005% (p = 1.00), while tissue explained 9.2%
(p = 0.002) and fish identity 37.7% (p = 0.002). Consistently, the mean Bray-Curtis
dissimilarity between the two sequencing runs of a shared library was 0.13, and
0.25 between libraries, against 0.88 between different fish of the same tissue and
0.94 across tissues (Fig. X). The two to three technical versions of each sample
therefore cluster tightly within the ordination relative to the spread among
individuals. Because every sample is present in all batches, technical and
biological factors are crossed rather than confounded, and batch correction was
unnecessary.


DISCUSSION — sentence on rare-taxon reproducibility

Repeated sequencing of the same amplicon library recovered only 48% of the ASVs
present at one to three reads in the other run, and Jaccard dissimilarity between
two sequencings of one library reached 0.45 against 0.12 for Bray-Curtis; rare taxa
are thus poorly reproducible even in the absence of any library-preparation step,
which is why we based diversity analyses on abundance-weighted metrics rather than
on observed richness or unweighted indices.
